Before you head out

This map shows where the habitat and weather look promising. It does not identify mushrooms and cannot tell you anything is safe to eat.

  • Never eat a wild mushroom unless someone experienced has confirmed the ID in person.
  • Picking rules change. Check the land manager's current rules on site.
  • Areas outside mapped public land are treated as private. Get permission first.

The Minnesota Mycological Society runs forays and ID sessions.

How this works

Habitat combines NLCD land cover (how much deciduous, mixed, or wooded-wetland forest is around) with USFS tree-species maps for host trees like white oak, aspen, elm and maple. The tree maps don't include red or bur oak, so oak habitat is underestimated in places.

Timing compares the season window, last week's temperatures, rain from 2–10 days ago, and soil moisture.

Access comes from PAD-US public lands, translated into picking rules.

Look mode counts any open public land. Harvest mode counts only land where personal-use picking is allowed, and half-counts land where rules vary.

Data sources

  • NLCD 2021 (MRLC) — land cover, 30 m
  • USFS FIA Forest Atlas — tree species density, 250 m
  • PAD-US — public and protected lands
  • Open-Meteo — weather and soil
  • iNaturalist — community sightings (locations of some are blurred for privacy)
